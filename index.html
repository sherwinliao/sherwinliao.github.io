About

My research examines how AI enables institutions to extend the boundaries of what they are authorised to say and do.

I am a Master of Global Media Communication graduate from the University of Melbourne. My master’s research examined how Chinese AI policy and state broadcasting construct technological legitimacy and institutional authority. My current work carries that question from representation toward AI-mediated services and interfaces, using digital health as a particularly revealing empirical setting. I am also developing a human–computer interaction direction that asks how shifts in authority and responsibility are enacted, understood, and potentially redesigned through interaction.

Current question How does AI enable institutions to extend what they are authorised to say and do?

Current extension How are these shifts in authority and responsibility enacted and understood through AI-mediated interaction?
Research

Research agenda

AI, institutional authority & digital health

My current research treats digital health as an empirical site for examining how non-clinical institutions such as platforms and insurers acquire new capacities to interpret concerns, recommend action, and route users toward care.

01

When institutions move beyond conventional roles

Institutional roles & AI authority

I examine how AI changes the boundaries of institutional roles: what an institution can legitimately communicate, recommend, organise, and act upon. My current comparative focus examines Afu within the Ant/Alipay ecosystem and nib’s AI-enabled health service as two forms of non-clinical health intermediation.

Institutional authorityPlatform governanceRole change
02

Tracing how authority is enacted through interfaces

Interface authority, routing & responsibility

I study how prompts, recommendations, disclaimers, source cues, professional expertise, service pathways, and human handoffs assemble institutional authority and redistribute responsibility through interaction.

Interface analysisResponsibilityAuthority handoffs
03

Extending a media question into interaction research

Authority in human–AI interaction

An emerging direction asks how users understand who is speaking, who is responsible, and when authority shifts between AI, platforms, insurers, and human services. This creates scope for focused user research and for testing clearer interaction and handoff designs.

Human-computer interactionUser understandingDesign evaluation
