About

My research examines how generative AI reshapes the communicative role and authority of digital intermediaries.

I am a Master of Global Media Communication graduate from the University of Melbourne. My master’s research examined how Chinese AI policy and state broadcasting construct technological legitimacy and institutional authority. My current work carries this question from state-media representation into generative AI platforms, asking how generative AI expands the communicative roles of digital intermediaries through knowledge interpretation, the organisation of action, and functions conventionally associated with institutions and professionals.

Current question How does generative AI reshape the communicative role and authority of digital intermediaries?

Current focus How does a digital platform organise generative knowledge communication, and under what conditions are its teacher-like explanations and evaluations presented as authoritative, questioned or recognised?
Research

Research agenda

Generative AI, communicative authority & digital intermediation

My research examines how generative AI changes the roles of digital intermediaries between institutions, information, services and publics. Rather than treating authority as an inherent technical property of AI, I study how communicative roles and claims to authority are performed through platform practices and interfaces, connected to institutional and professional sources, recognised, qualified or contested by others, and bounded by formal authority.

Primary project · proposed doctoral research

Doubao Aixue — Communicative Roles and Teacher-like Authority

This proposed doctoral project examines Doubao Aixue as a focal learner-facing service situated within the wider Doubao platform environment. It investigates how curriculum organisation, responsive explanations, feedback and evaluation are combined in learning interactions, and how these communicative activities construct and support claims to authority. The study examines how those claims are challenged, bounded and discussed through interfaces, platform discourse and wider public communication.

Doubao Aixue model filing
RQ1

Communicative Roles

How does Doubao Aixue organise generative communication within learner-facing interaction, and what communicative roles are enacted through this organisation?

RQ2

Authority and Boundaries

How are claims to communicative authority constructed, challenged and bounded through interfaces, interactions and formal platform discourse?

RQ3

Circulation and Negotiation

How are these roles and authority claims articulated, circulated and negotiated across platform, editorial media, creator and public discourse?

Doctoral contribution

The project examines how platform-organised learning pathways and context-responsive knowledge communication jointly configure claims to communicative authority. It distinguishes observable interaction from unverified technical mechanisms, institutional claims from documented authority recognition, and researcher-led challenges from actual public responses. Its contribution is to explain how communicative roles and their authority boundaries are configured and negotiated within a platform-organised generative service.

Emerging direction

Healthdirect × ChatGPT — Institutional Authority Across Platform Boundaries

An emerging independent research direction examining how public-health institutional authority may be preserved, transferred and reconfigured when trusted health information and clinical guidance move into a third-party generative AI environment. Healthdirect Australia's announced ChatGPT initiative provides a setting for studying the visibility of institutional identity, evidence, safeguards, accountability and escalation to human clinicians across platform boundaries.

Core analytical mechanismCross-platform transfer and re-anchoring of institutional authority

Potential direction

DAISY — Authoritative Interpretation Without Decision-making Power

A potential independent study of how a local-government generative AI assistant interprets planning requirements and guides residents towards formal development applications without possessing statutory decision-making authority. The inquiry asks how explanations acquire practical significance, how their grounds and limitations are communicated, and how interpretive guidance remains separated from decisions made by council planning staff.

Core analytical mechanismSeparation of interpretive authority and formal decision-making authority

Independent pilot foundation

Shen Xiao i (深小i) — Institutional Delegation and Procedural Authority Boundaries

An independent public-sector pilot examining how an already authoritative government institution delegates policy explanation, service guidance and selected procedural activities to an AI assistant. The focus is on how institutional voice and responsibility are communicated through AI-mediated interactions, where official authorisation begins and ends, and how human oversight and formal administrative authority remain connected to automated services.

Core analytical mechanismInstitutionally delegated communication and bounded procedural action
