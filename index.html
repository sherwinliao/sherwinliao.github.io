About

My research examines how generative AI reshapes the communicative role and authority of digital intermediaries.

I am a Master of Global Media Communication graduate from the University of Melbourne. My master’s research examined how Chinese AI policy and state broadcasting construct technological legitimacy and institutional authority. My current work carries this question from state-media representation into platform-organised generative communication, examining how digital intermediaries organise knowledge sources, perform specialised roles and define the limits of their explanations and advice.

Current question How does generative AI reshape the communicative role and authority of digital intermediaries?

Proposed doctoral research

Doubao Aixue & Xiaohe AI Doctor — Communicative Roles and Authority Claims

This proposed doctoral project examines a bounded set of ByteDance’s Chinese AI services. Doubao Aixue and Xiaohe AI Doctor form the two core analytical units, comparing educational and health-related communication. The main Doubao app provides a common reference for source organisation, general-purpose explanation and action routing in corresponding scenarios. The study asks how specialised roles and claims to professional authority are configured through interaction, platform design and governance, and how they are negotiated in public discourse.

Doubao Aixue model filing · Xiaohe AI Doctor model filing
RQ1

Communicative Roles

How do general-purpose and specialised AI services organise sources, explanations, judgements and subsequent action, and what communicative roles are enacted through these arrangements?

RQ2

Authority and Governance Boundaries

How are claims to educational and medical authority supported, challenged and bounded through interactions, interfaces and governance documents, in relation to the general-purpose reference service?

RQ3

Circulation and Negotiation

How are these communicative roles and authority claims articulated, circulated and negotiated across official platform, editorial media, creator and public discourse?

Research contribution

The project brings platform intermediation, AI-mediated explanation and professional authority into an embedded comparison of educational and health services. It examines how source visibility, role performance and responsibility boundaries align or diverge within one platform environment. Claims to expertise are distinguished from documented recognition, and visible interactions from unverified backend mechanisms.

Research approach

The proposed qualitative-dominant mixed-methods design combines repeated structured walkthroughs in matched scenarios, authority challenges, longitudinal researcher documentation and adult critical-incident interviews with descriptive content analysis and critical discourse analysis.
