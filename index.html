About

My research examines how AI enables institutions to extend the boundaries of what they are authorised to say and do.

I am a Master of Global Media Communication graduate from the University of Melbourne. My master’s research examined how Chinese AI policy and state broadcasting construct technological legitimacy and institutional authority. My current work carries that question from state-media representation into platform-mediated services, comparing how major Chinese digital platforms use AI health interfaces to extend established roles in information, communication, and content distribution into health interpretation, recommendation, and service routing.

Current question How does AI enable institutions to extend what they are authorised to say and do?

Current focus How is this extension enacted as digital platforms use AI to interpret health concerns, recommend action, and route users toward professional services?
Research

Research agenda

AI, institutional authority & platform-mediated health

My current research treats AI-mediated health as an empirical site for examining how major Chinese digital platforms extend beyond their established informational and communicative roles. I compare how AI health applications allow platforms to interpret user concerns, organise medical knowledge, recommend action, and route users toward professional services.

01

When platforms extend beyond established media and information roles

Platform roles & AI authority

I compare Baidu, Tencent, and ByteDance as three major Chinese digital platforms with different histories in search, social communication, and content distribution. The study asks how AI enables each platform to extend into health interpretation, recommendation, and service routing, and what forms of institutional authority become possible in the process.

Institutional authorityPlatform governanceRole extension
02

Tracing how authority is enacted through interfaces

Interface authority, routing & responsibility

I study how prompts, AI-generated summaries, source labels, medical credentials, risk warnings, recommendations, and links to human services assemble authority and distribute responsibility across the interface.

Interface analysisResponsibilityAuthority handoffs
03

Comparing different platform pathways into health

Platform infrastructures & institutional extension

Rather than treating AI health assistants as equivalent, I examine how each platform’s existing infrastructure, ecosystem position, and service connections shape what its AI can say, recommend, and route users toward.

Chinese digital platformsPlatform infrastructuresComparative analysis

Comparative research pathway

From platform roles to AI-mediated health authority

  1. 01

    Trace

    Map each platform’s conventional communicative role, institutional position, and health-service boundaries.

  2. 02

    Walk through

    Run standardised health scenarios and document prompts, outputs, source cues, disclaimers, and service pathways.

  3. 03

    Compare

    Code how the platforms interpret concerns, recommend action, claim or defer authority, and route users onward.

  4. 04

    Explain

    Relate recurring differences to platform infrastructures, ecosystem ties, and institutional role extension.
