About

My research examines how generative AI reshapes the communicative role and authority of digital intermediaries.

I am a Master of Global Media Communication graduate from the University of Melbourne. My master’s research examined how Chinese AI policy and state broadcasting construct technological legitimacy and institutional authority. My current work carries this question from state-media representation into generative AI platforms, asking what happens when digital intermediaries begin not only to distribute information, but to interpret knowledge, organise action, and assume roles previously associated with institutions and professionals.

Current question How does generative AI reshape the communicative role and authority of digital intermediaries?

Current focus How do generative AI platforms construct and perform pedagogical authority as they interpret knowledge, organise learning, and assume teacher-like roles?
Research

Research agenda

Generative AI, communicative authority & digital intermediation

My research examines how generative AI changes the role of digital intermediaries between institutions, information, services, and publics. Rather than treating communicative authority as a property of the model or as something only formally granted by institutions, I study how it is constructed through platform practices, inherited from trusted institutions and professional knowledge, performed through interfaces, bounded by formal authority, and redistributed across human and AI actors.

01

Platform roles & communicative authority

How digital intermediaries acquire authority

I examine how communicative authority is constructed through platform practices, inherited from institutions and professional knowledge, and delegated across generative AI services.

02

Interface performance, action & authority boundaries

How authority is performed, extended into action, and bounded

I trace how generated explanations, feedback, recommendations, sequencing and service routing move AI-mediated communication from interpretation toward action, while interface cues, disclaimers and human escalation mark the limits of formal and professional authority.

Interface analysisService routingAuthority boundaries
03

Media circulation & public rearticulation

How AI roles change as they circulate

I examine how AI-mediated roles are articulated, represented, renamed, extended, negotiated, or contested as they circulate across platform interfaces, media ecosystems and public discourse.
